# The Echoes of Tomorrow: The Prophecies of Alaundo

At the heart of Candlekeep's spiritual life is the **Endless Chant**, a rhythmic recitation of the prophecies of Alaundo the Seer that has continued uninterrupted for over a thousand years.

## Alaundo the Seer
Alaundo arrived at Candlekeep in 75 DR, a man whose mind was already half-consumed by the visions of the future. He spent his final days recording his prophecies in nine massive volumes. To the uninitiated, his writings appear as a chaotic jumble of poetry, marginalia, and cryptic sketches. To the Avowed, they are the blueprint of destiny.

## The Endless Chant
In a courtyard within the Inner Ward, a group of thirty monks led by the **Chanter** performs the recitation. They move in a slow, circular procession, their voices rising and falling in a hypnotic cadence.
*   **The Voices:** The Chanter is accompanied by the **Voices of the North, South, and East**. Together, they ensure that every word Alaundo wrote is spoken aloud at least once every few days.
*   **The Fulfillment:** When a prophecy is undeniably fulfilled, it is removed from the Chant. The monks believe that by speaking the unfulfilled prophecies, they are anchoring them to reality, ensuring that the timeline remains stable.

## The Prophecy of the End
The most chilling of Alaundo's visions concerns the ultimate fate of Faerûn. Scholars estimate the date of this event to be **2163 DR**. The prophecy speaks of:
> *"A Serpent to blow a Horn of Doom at the Graveyard Kingdom of Man, when the sun turns to ash and the stars weep blood."*

While many dismiss this as the ramblings of a dying man, the staggering accuracy of Alaundo's other predictions—such as the fall of the Netherese or the coming of the Spellplague—makes the "Horn of Doom" a source of constant, low-level dread among the high-ranking Avowed.

## Interpreting the Seer
Alaundo's prophecies are notoriously difficult to interpret before they happen. They are often metaphorical and only become clear in hindsight. For example, his prophecy about "The Iron Bird that Swallows the Sun" was only understood after the first gnomish airship crashed into a temple of Amaunator. This ambiguity is why the Avowed spend so much time studying the texts, hoping to find a warning they can actually use.
