# The Order of the Avowed: Guardians of the Flame

The Avowed are not a traditional monastic order. They are a collection of scholars, scribes, and guardians united by their devotion to knowledge. Their hierarchy is rigid, designed to ensure that the library functions with the precision of a clockwork machine.

## The High Council
At the top of the hierarchy are the leaders who make the strategic decisions for Candlekeep.
*   **The Keeper of Tomes:** The ultimate executive authority. Their word is absolute law within the fortress. They oversee the administration, the finances, and the defense of the keep.
*   **The First Reader:** The primary scholar of Candlekeep. While the Keeper handles the "how," the First Reader handles the "what." They decide which books are acquired and how they are categorized.
*   **The Eight Great Readers:** A council of elders who oversee the various departments of the library. Each is a world-renowned expert in a specific field, from Arcana to Zoology.

## The Working Ranks
*   **Master Readers:** The teachers and mentors. They oversee the education of the acolytes and the work of the scribes.
*   **Scribes:** The backbone of the order. They spend their days copying texts, translating ancient languages, and repairing damaged scrolls. It is a life of quiet, repetitive labor, but one of immense importance.
*   **Acolytes:** The lowest rank. They wear black robes and perform the manual labor required to keep the fortress running—cleaning, cooking, and carrying books. Only after years of service and rigorous testing can an acolyte be promoted to a Scribe.

## The Guardians of the Gate
Because Candlekeep holds secrets that could topple kingdoms, its security is paramount.
*   **The Gatewarden:** Responsible for the physical defense of the keep and the vetting of all visitors.
*   **The Watchers:** Armed monks who patrol the walls and courtyards. They wear plain brown robes and are trained in both martial arts and the use of staves.
*   **The Keeper of the Emerald Door:** A position of immense trust, as they control access to the Inner Ward and the most sensitive parts of the collection.

## The Politics of Silence
While the Avowed are non-denominational, they are not without internal friction. There are constant, subtle debates between the "Preservationists," who believe the library should be a closed vault, and the "Disseminators," who believe knowledge should be shared more freely with the world. These arguments are never loud, but they shape the policies of the keep for generations.
