# The Eternal Struggle: Politics and the Blood War

The Nine Hells is defined by two great conflicts: the external war against the Abyss, and the internal war for status and power.

## The Blood War: The Great Filter
For as long as there has been law and chaos, there has been the Blood War. It is a conflict of such scale that it beggars the mortal imagination. Every day, millions of demons pour out of the Abyss, driven by a primal urge to destroy everything in their path. And every day, the legions of Hell stand in their way.

The devils view themselves as the only thing standing between the multiverse and total annihilation. In their eyes, every soul they harvest and every atrocity they commit is justified by the necessity of holding the line at Avernus. If the Hells were to fall, the demons would sweep across the Material Plane like a locust swarm, leaving nothing but dust and silence. This "noble" cause is the ultimate shield Asmodeus uses to justify his existence to the gods.

## The Court of Nessus: A Den of Vipers
Internal politics in the Hells is a game of "The Rule of Law" taken to a lethal extreme. Every devil is constantly plotting to overthrow their superior, but they must do so within the framework of the law. To kill a superior is a crime; to orchestrate their failure so spectacularly that Asmodeus himself demotes them is a masterpiece.

### The Great Game of the Archdukes
The rulers of the nine layers are in a state of perpetual, cold competition. They form alliances, sign treaties, and betray one another with a grace that would put any mortal court to shame.
*   **Mephistopheles** plots to replace Asmodeus through arcane supremacy.
*   **Baalzebul** seeks to regain his lost beauty and status through manipulation.
*   **Dispater** hides in his tower, certain that everyone is plotting against him (and he is usually right).
*   **Zariel** cares only for the war, viewing the political maneuvering of her peers with contempt.

## The Role of the Mortal
In this grand game, mortals are the "points" on the scoreboard. A devil's status is measured by the number and quality of the souls they have brought to the Hells. A peasant's soul is a copper piece; a hero's soul is a diamond. This is why devils are so invested in mortal affairs. They are not just looking for evil; they are looking for *significant* evil. They want the kings, the high priests, and the legendary champions. Because in the Nine Hells, the one with the most souls doesn't just win the game—they become the law.
