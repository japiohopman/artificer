# What Is a Spell?

Magic is everywhere.

It flows through the world in unseen currents, woven into the fabric of the multiverse. Those who learn to wield it can reach into these invisible strands, shape them into patterns, and release them as something extraordinary.

A **spell** is one such expression of magic—a carefully shaped effect with a specific purpose.

A spellcaster might pluck at the hidden threads of magic, hold them in a precise pattern, set them vibrating in a particular way, and then release them.

In the space of a few heartbeats, the impossible becomes real.

A spell might become a weapon, a shield, a deception, or a blessing. It can burn an enemy, mend a wound, protect an ally, alter the world around you, remove a terrible affliction, or even return the dead to life.

Thousands upon thousands of spells have been created throughout the history of the multiverse.

Many have been lost.

Some may still lie forgotten in crumbling spellbooks, sealed within ancient ruins, hidden in the memories of dead gods, or waiting to be rediscovered by a spellcaster powerful and wise enough to uncover them.

And perhaps one day, **you will create one of your own.**

---

# Spell Levels

Every spell has a **spell level from 0 to 9**.

A spell's level is a general measure of its magical power.

A simple **Magic Missile** may be cast at 1st level, while **Wish**, one of the most powerful spells known, reaches the 9th level of magic.

The higher a spell's level, the greater the mastery required to wield it.

Spell level and character level are not the same thing. A character must typically reach **17th level** before they can command the might of a 9th-level spell.

### Cantrips

**Cantrips** are the simplest spells, yet even these small workings can be remarkably powerful.

A cantrip is a **0-level spell** that a spellcaster has practiced so thoroughly that they can cast it almost by instinct.

---

# Known & Prepared Spells

Before a spellcaster can cast a spell, that magic must somehow be within their reach.

Some spellcasters, such as **bards and sorcerers**, learn a limited selection of spells that remain firmly fixed in their minds.

Other spellcasters, such as **clerics and wizards**, prepare their spells before setting out, choosing which pieces of their magical knowledge they are ready to call upon.

The exact method varies from class to class.

How many spells a character can know or prepare depends on their **level and class**.

---

# Spell Slots

Knowing a spell does not mean you can cast it endlessly.

Magic demands a price.

Shaping the raw power of the multiverse and forcing it into a spell requires both **mental focus and physical energy**. More powerful spells place an even greater strain upon the caster.

To represent this, spellcasters have a limited number of **spell slots**.

Think of a spell slot as a reservoir of magical energy.

A 1st-level spell requires a 1st-level slot, while a more powerful spell requires a larger reservoir. A lower-level spell can be cast using a higher-level slot, but a higher-level spell cannot be forced into a smaller one.

For example, a 3rd-level wizard might have:

* **Four 1st-level spell slots**
* **Two 2nd-level spell slots**

If the wizard casts *Magic Missile*, a 1st-level spell, using one of those 1st-level slots, one slot is expended and three remain.

A **long rest** restores expended spell slots.

Some abilities allow characters or creatures to cast certain spells without using spell slots at all.

---

# Casting at a Higher Level

Sometimes a spellcaster can pour more magical energy into a spell than it normally requires.

When you cast a spell using a slot of a **higher level**, the spell is treated as being cast at that higher level.

For example, if a wizard casts *Magic Missile* using a 2nd-level spell slot, the spell becomes a **2nd-level casting**.

Some spells become significantly more powerful when cast this way. Their descriptions explain exactly what happens when additional magical energy is poured into them.

The magic expands to fill the power available to it.

---

# Cantrips

Cantrips are spells that can be cast **at will**.

They do not require a spell slot and do not need to be prepared in advance.

Through repeated practice, a cantrip becomes so deeply familiar to its caster that the magic can be called upon almost instinctively.

A flick of the fingers.

A whispered word.

A spark of power.

Then the spell is done.

Cantrips are **0-level spells**, but don't mistake simplicity for weakness.

A well-used cantrip can save a life—or end one.

---

# Rituals

Some spells carry the **Ritual** tag.

A spellcaster capable of performing rituals can cast such a spell without expending a spell slot.

The price is time.

Casting a spell as a ritual takes **10 minutes longer** than normal, but allows the caster to preserve their precious spell slots for when they are truly needed.

A ritual cannot be cast at a higher level.

To cast a spell as a ritual, the spellcaster must have a feature that allows ritual casting and must have the spell prepared or know it, unless their class feature states otherwise.

Clerics and druids, for example, can gain the ability to cast certain spells as rituals, while wizards have their own special rules for ritual magic.

## The Art of Magic

Every spell begins as an idea.

A pattern.

A whisper of power waiting to be shaped.

Some spells have been mastered by generations of spellcasters. Others have been forgotten. Still others have yet to be discovered.

**Magic is not merely something you cast.**

**It is something you learn, shape, and ultimately make your own.**
