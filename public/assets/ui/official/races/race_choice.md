# Choosing Your Species

Every character belongs to a **species**, one of the many intelligent peoples that inhabit the world of Dungeons & Dragons. Your species is more than a name or a shape—it is part of your character's heritage, shaping their appearance, natural talents, and the traditions they carry with them.

The most common adventurers are **dwarves, elves, halflings, and humans**, but the world is home to many other peoples as well. **Dragonborn, gnomes, half-elves, half-orcs, and tieflings** all have their own histories, cultures, and ways of seeing the world. Some species are further divided into distinct ancestries or subraces, such as the **mountain dwarf** or **wood elf**.

Your choice of species helps define where your character comes from—and gives you the first clues about who they might become.

---

### What Your Species Gives You

Your ancestry grants a number of traits shaped by generations of heritage, culture, and natural ability.

* **Biological Traits**
  Innate abilities such as Darkvision, natural resistances, keen senses, or magical talents.

* **Ability Score Increases**
  Natural strengths and inclinations inherited through your ancestry.

* **Size & Speed**
  Your physical stature and natural movement, affecting how you move through the world and across the battlefield.

* **Languages & Culture**
  Languages you know and traditions passed down through generations.

* **Special Proficiencies**
  Training and knowledge associated with your people, such as proficiency with certain weapons, tools, or skills.

---

### Your Heritage, Your Choice

Some species naturally complement certain classes. A **lightfoot halfling**, for example, can make an exceptional rogue, while a **high elf** may possess many of the qualities associated with a powerful wizard.

But these are only traditions—not rules.

A mountain dwarf might abandon the forge to study the arcane arts. A half-orc might devote their life to a sacred oath and become a paladin. The most memorable adventurers are often those who refuse to fit the mold expected of them.
