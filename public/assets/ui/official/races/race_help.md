# Choosing Your Species

Humans are among the most common people in the worlds of **Dungeons & Dragons**, but they are far from alone. They share the world with dwarves, elves, halflings, and countless other fantastic peoples.

Your character belongs to one of these peoples.

Not every intelligent species in the multiverse is suited to become a player-controlled adventurer. **Dwarves, elves, halflings, and humans** are among the most common heroes found in adventuring parties. **Dragonborn, gnomes, half-elves, half-orcs, and tieflings** are less common, while some peoples—such as the **drow**—are rarer still.

Your choice of species affects many aspects of your character. It establishes fundamental qualities that remain with you throughout your adventuring career, from your natural abilities and physical characteristics to the languages you speak and the traditions you carry.

When making this choice, consider the kind of character you want to play. A halfling might make a nimble and elusive rogue. A dwarf can be a formidable warrior, while an elf may possess a natural talent for arcane magic.

But these are only possibilities—not limitations.

A character's species can suggest a path, but it never determines who that character must become.

---

## Your Species, Your Story

Your species influences more than your statistics and racial traits. It can also provide the first clues for building your character's story.

Each species has its own history, appearance, customs, social traditions, and common personality traits. Some cultures may have tendencies toward particular alignments or ways of life.

These details are **guidelines, not rules**.

Your dwarf might reject the traditions of their clan. Your elf might have little interest in the customs of their people. Your tiefling might embrace a life far removed from the expectations placed upon them.

In fact, asking **why your character is different** can be one of the best ways to discover their personality, motivations, and background.

Your heritage gives you a beginning.

**What you make of it is your story.**

# Racial Traits

Each species grants a collection of **racial traits** shared by its members. These traits represent the natural abilities, characteristics, and heritage of your people.

### Ability Score Increase

Every species increases one or more of your character's **ability scores**, reflecting natural strengths commonly associated with that ancestry.

### Age

This entry describes when members of the species typically reach adulthood and how long they may live.

Your character can be any age that makes sense for their story. Age can even help explain certain characteristics—for example, a particularly young or elderly character might have less physical strength, while years of experience could contribute to greater knowledge or wisdom.

### Alignment

Many species and cultures have tendencies toward certain **alignments**.

These tendencies are not rules.

A dwarf raised in a deeply lawful society might nevertheless become chaotic. An elf might reject the traditions of their people entirely. Thinking about *why* your character differs from those expectations can reveal something important about who they are.

### Size

Most player characters are **Medium**, representing creatures roughly 4 to 8 feet tall.

Some species are **Small**, generally standing between 2 and 4 feet tall. Size can affect certain aspects of gameplay, including how a character interacts with equipment and weapons.

### Speed

Your **speed** determines how far your character can move while exploring the world and during combat.

### Languages

Your heritage determines which **languages** your character can speak, read, and write.

The wider world contains many languages, from those spoken across great kingdoms to ancient tongues known only by a handful of peoples.

### Subraces

Some species are divided into **subraces**. A subrace retains the traits of its parent species while gaining additional traits of its own.

The relationships between subraces can differ greatly between cultures and worlds.

For example, in the **Dragonlance** setting, mountain dwarves and hill dwarves may live together as different clans of the same people. In the **Forgotten Realms**, however, related dwarf peoples have developed separate kingdoms, cultures, and identities.

Your heritage is therefore shaped not only by what you are—but also by **where you come from**.
