# Rogue

A halfling signals for her companions to wait.

She creeps quietly down the dungeon corridor, presses an ear against a heavy wooden door, and listens. Then she draws a small set of tools from her pack. A few careful movements later, the lock clicks open.

She slips back into the shadows just as her fighter companion steps forward and kicks the door wide.

Rogues rely on **skill, stealth, and cunning** to gain the upper hand. They are masters of finding opportunities where others see obstacles, exploiting weaknesses where others see strength, and solving problems with ingenuity rather than brute force.

A rogue can be a scout, burglar, assassin, investigator, swindler, treasure hunter, or something entirely different.

Whatever path they choose, rogues share one important quality:

**They find a way.**

---

## Skill & Precision

Rogues devote themselves to mastering a remarkable variety of skills, developing a breadth of expertise few adventurers can match.

Some become masters of **stealth and deception**, slipping past guards or convincing enemies that they were never there at all. Others specialize in the challenges of ancient ruins and dangerous dungeons—climbing walls, finding hidden traps, disarming mechanisms, and opening locks.

When combat begins, rogues favor **cunning over brute force**.

Rather than overwhelming an enemy with a storm of attacks, a rogue looks for the perfect opening. One precise strike, delivered exactly where it hurts most, can be worth more than a dozen wild swings.

Rogues also have an uncanny talent for escaping danger. Their quick reflexes and instincts allow them to slip away from threats that would catch other adventurers off guard.

Some even learn a little magic to complement their other talents.

---

## A Shady Life

Every town and city has its rogues.

Some live up to the darker reputation of the profession, making their living as **burglars, assassins, cutpurses, and con artists**. Many operate within thieves' guilds, criminal organizations, or powerful crime families.

Others work alone.

And not every rogue is a criminal.

Some make an honest living as **locksmiths, investigators, scouts, or even exterminators**—although in a world where dire rats and wererats lurk beneath the streets, even honest work can become dangerous.

Among adventurers, rogues can be found on either side of the law.

Some are hardened criminals searching for their next fortune in forgotten treasure. Others become adventurers to escape the law—or the people they once worked for.

Some deliberately train for the profession, mastering the skills needed to infiltrate ancient ruins, navigate hidden crypts, and recover treasures that others cannot reach.

For a rogue, the difference between **crime and adventure** can sometimes be little more than who owns the treasure.

## Creating a Rogue

When creating your rogue, consider what brought them to the life of adventure.

### What Is Your Relationship With the Law?

Do you have a criminal past—or a criminal present?

Are you running from the law, or from an angry thieves' guild master?

Perhaps you left your guild willingly, searching for greater risks and greater rewards.

Or maybe you have never belonged to a guild at all.

### What Drives You?

Is it **greed**?

The thrill of danger?

A desire for freedom?

Revenge?

Curiosity?

Perhaps you simply believe there is always another door to open and another secret waiting to be discovered.

### Why Did You Leave Your Old Life?

Maybe a great con or heist went terribly wrong.

Perhaps one successful robbery earned you enough coin to escape the life you were living.

Maybe wanderlust finally pulled you away from home.

You could have been separated from your family or mentor and forced to find a new way to survive.

Or perhaps another adventurer saw something in you—another way to use your talents, another life you had never considered.

Whatever the reason, something happened that changed the direction of your life.

**That moment is part of your story.**

## Quick Build

You can create a rogue quickly by following these suggestions.

First, make **Dexterity** your highest ability score. It improves many of your most important rogue abilities, including your attacks, defense, and agility.

Make **Intelligence** your next-highest ability if you want to excel at Investigation or pursue the **Arcane Trickster** archetype.

Choose **Charisma** instead if you want to emphasize deception, persuasion, and social interaction.

For your background, **Charlatan** is a natural fit for a rogue who lives by deception and misdirection.

# Rogue Features

As a rogue, you gain the following class features.

### Hit Points

**Hit Dice:** 1d8 per rogue level

**Hit Points at 1st Level:** 8 + your Constitution modifier

**Hit Points at Higher Levels:** 1d8 (or 5) + your Constitution modifier per rogue level after 1st

### Proficiencies

**Armor:** Light armor

**Weapons:** Simple weapons, hand crossbows, longswords, rapiers, shortswords

**Tools:** Thieves' tools

**Saving Throws:** Dexterity, Intelligence

**Skills:** Choose four from Acrobatics, Athletics, Deception, Insight, Intimidation, Investigation, Perception, Performance, Persuasion, Sleight of Hand, and Stealth.
