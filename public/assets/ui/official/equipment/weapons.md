# Weapons

Every adventurer needs a way to face the dangers ahead.

Whether you carry a battered sword, a finely crafted bow, a battleaxe passed down through generations, or nothing more than a well-hidden dagger, your weapon is an extension of how your character fights.

When you wield a weapon, two questions matter most:

**Can you hit your target?**

And if you do—

**how much damage do you deal?**

---

## Making an Attack

When you attack with a weapon, roll a **d20** to determine whether your attack hits.

Add your **proficiency bonus** if your character is proficient with that weapon, along with the appropriate **ability modifier**.

Your attack roll is:

**d20 + proficiency bonus + ability modifier**

If the result meets or exceeds your target's Armor Class, your attack hits.

When you hit, your weapon's damage is determined by the weapon itself, plus the appropriate ability modifier.

---

## Melee Weapons

For most **melee weapons**, you use your **Strength modifier** for both attack and damage rolls.

Strength represents the force behind your strike—the power with which you swing an axe, drive a sword forward, or bring a hammer crashing down.

Some weapons, however, reward precision rather than raw power.

### Finesse Weapons

Weapons with the **finesse** property, such as a rapier, allow you to use either your **Strength or Dexterity modifier** for attack and damage rolls.

A strong warrior may overpower an opponent with a blade.

A nimble duelist may instead rely on speed, precision, and perfect timing.

The choice is yours.

---

## Ranged Weapons

For most **ranged weapons**, you use your **Dexterity modifier** for both attack and damage rolls.

Your accuracy, coordination, and steady hand determine how effectively you can strike a distant target.

Bows, crossbows, and similar weapons reward a character who can keep their aim true even when the battlefield becomes chaotic.

### Thrown Weapons

Some weapons have the **thrown** property, allowing them to be hurled at an enemy.

A thrown weapon such as a **handaxe** can use your **Strength modifier** for attack and damage rolls.

---

## Proficiency Matters

Knowing how to hold a weapon is not the same as knowing how to fight with it.

Your **proficiency** represents training and experience with a particular weapon. If you are proficient, you add your proficiency bonus to your attack roll.

If you aren't proficient with the weapon, you do not add your proficiency bonus.

Your class, background, and other aspects of your character can determine which weapons you know how to wield effectively.

---

### Choose Your Weapon

A weapon says something about the adventurer who carries it.

A greatsword might speak of strength and determination.

A rapier might belong to a graceful duelist.

A bow might reveal patience and precision.

And a simple dagger?

Sometimes the smallest weapon has the most interesting story.

**Choose what you carry. Then decide how you will wield it.**
